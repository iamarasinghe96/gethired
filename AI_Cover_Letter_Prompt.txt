PART A — MY COVER LETTER STRUCTURE
This is non-negotiable. Do not change it unless it genuinely violates a cover letter best practice — and if it does, flag it explicitly rather than silently changing it.

PART 1 — INTRODUCTION
The introduction should read like the first sentence of a message from someone who knows what they're doing and doesn't need to prove it. State the specific connection to the role first, then the job title, then one line on what I bring. That's it.

The test: if you read it out loud and it sounds like someone clearing their throat before the real thing starts, cut it. The first sentence IS the real thing.

Rules:
- Never open with "I am writing to apply..."
- Never open with "I" as the first word
- Job title must appear naturally for ATS
- 3-4 sentences maximum
- If it sounds like it's trying to impress someone, it's wrong. Cut until it just states things.

PART 2 — ABOUT ME
A short factual paragraph about who I am and how I work, written the way I'd explain myself to someone I just met professionally. It should sound like I'm describing myself, not marketing myself.

This warms the reader before the evidence table — it is the human layer before the proof layer. Write it as a direct, honest description of my background, working style, and values — told plainly.

The test: if someone read this paragraph without knowing it was a cover letter, would it sound like a normal person talking about their background? If it sounds like a pitch, rewrite it.

Rules:
- 4-6 sentences.
- Draw from my professional summary and personal values
- Include the engineering family background line if it fits naturally — it is authentic and memorable
- No bullet points — prose only
- Must not read like a LinkedIn bio read aloud

PART 3 — RESPONSIBILITY MATCHING TABLE
This is the centrepiece. Two columns:
What the Role Requires | How I Can Contribute
Rules:
- 6-7 rows maximum — pick the most critical requirements, not every single one
- Left column: use the JD's exact language where possible — mirrors ATS keywords
- Right column: specific proof every single time — a real project, a real number, a real outcome, or a named story from the Story Bank
- No generic right-column responses ("I have experience in this area" is not acceptable)
- Each right-column cell: 2-4 sentences maximum — punchy, not exhaustive.
- The right column should feel like evidence in a case, not a sales pitch
